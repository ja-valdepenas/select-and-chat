Three directions for the Select & Chat icon

All three drop the emerald palette for the app's own yellow-green, the #68A500 seed the whole interface is built from, and all three try to say selection, not just messaging. Pick one, or take parts from two.

What the current icon is doing

  • It is emerald; the app is yellow-green. The launcher art runs #34D399 → #047857, while every screen inside runs the #68A500 tonal palette. Icon and app do not look like the same product.
  • A green tile with a white bubble is the WhatsApp silhouette. At 48dp the resemblance is the whole read, and the icon is one of the three surfaces Meta's guidelines actually restrict. It is the reason the app is not called "Select & WhatsApp-it".
  • Nothing in it is about selecting. A bubble with two bars is the generic messaging mark. The one thing this app does that no other does, turning a highlighted number into a chat, is absent.

Highlight

The gesture itself: a number, selected.

Android's two selection handles clamp a highlighted run of text. That is the exact moment the app lives in: the half-second after a long-press, before the toolbar appears. No other icon on the phone is built from it. The handles are drawn to the platform's real geometry: a circle with one squared corner, hung off the ends of the selection.

The pale pill is the highlight, the dark bar inside it is the number, and the small tail between the handles settles what it becomes. Read quickly it is a chip of selected text; read slowly it is a message. Lime field, olive ink: maximum contrast at small size, and about as far from emerald as green gets.

Hand-off

The line you were reading. The chat it became.

A line of ordinary text sits at the bottom, its first stretch lit up in hot lime. That is the number you selected, and the bubble above it points its tail straight back down at that highlight. One picture, two beats: this bit of a page → that chat.

It is the only one of the three that explains the product to someone who has never seen it, which matters in a store listing next to four competitors. The dark olive field is the app's own default theme, so the icon and the first screen share a background.

Drop

One shape, doing both jobs at once.

A selection handle and a speech-bubble tail are the same object drawn twice: a circle with one corner pulled square. Draw it once, big, and the mark is a handle and a bubble at the same time. The corner points up and to the left, back at the text it came from, which is the whole relationship in one angle.

One shape and one bar, so nothing falls off: it survives every mask, holds at a 24dp notification size, and reduces to a single silhouette for the themed icon without losing a part. On the near-black field it matches the AMOLED theme the store screenshots are shot in.
